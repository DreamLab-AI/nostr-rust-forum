# ADR-2031 — Federated groups have one home relay, pinned by pubkey, and mirrors that replicate its signed state verbatim

## Context

Depends on ADR-2030. NIP-29 puts a group on one relay; the surveyed NIP-29 relay has no federation (a roadmap line only), so the design is ours.
The mesh carries gift-wrapped IS-Envelopes between DIDs, not group state; its default `federated_kinds` and inbound peer gate exclude kind 9, 9000-9022 and 39000-39003. `PeerConnector` has no implementor, so no event leaves a Worker today.
Addressable replacement keeps a late older event and a same-second tie beside the newer row (`storage.rs:97-139`). Every event older than seven days is refused. The relay has no signing key (ADR-2030).
The cron runs every five minutes (`wrangler.toml:123`); a Durable Object alarm exists (`relay_do/mod.rs:371`).
A mirror that withholds a removal, a home that is backdated, or a home that re-signs a stale projection would keep a removed member in. Round 1 set R = 60 s; round 2 found that clock incompatible with a 70 s stale threshold and with bumped timestamps, and replaced it with an admission `rev` and one end-to-end bound D = 90 s (AUDIT-DECISIONS C1, C2, C3).
Archived ADR-073, 074, 075 and 080 are rationale only; what is relied on is restated below.
Evidence: VisionClaw `docs/proposals/spatial-rooms/PLAN.md`, `research/02`, `AUDIT-DECISIONS.md` (B1, B2, M5, M8).

## Decision

Proposed:

1. **A space is `(home relay pubkey, group id)`, and the operator pins the home key (audit M8).** It is written `host'id` for NIP-29 interop but pinned by the home's lowercase 64-hex pubkey. The operator sets that pin in configuration, out of band. A relay never trusts a key on first use from NIP-11. The home advertises its key as the NIP-11 `self` field ("relay's own pubkey", independent of the administrator) for discovery only; a mismatch with the pin is an error, never an update. NIP-11 `pubkey` is the administrative contact and is never used as the home key. Renaming the host does not change the space. A fork under a new home key is a different space.
2. **The home is the only writer of group state and orders by a monotonic `rev` (audit B1, round-2 C1).**
   - The home keeps an admission sequence per group: a durable, monotonically increasing `u64`, the group-state revision. It applies moderation events 9000-9022 in its own admission order and authorises each against the state current at admission, then increments `rev`.
   - Every 39000-39003 carries `["rev", <u64>]`, the revision of the state it reflects. State order is by `rev`. A re-sign (heartbeat) repeats the same `rev` and identical content with a later `created_at`, so replacement and watermarks order by the pair (`rev`, `created_at`): a greater `rev` always wins; an equal `rev` wins only with a strictly greater `created_at` and identical tag content. An equal-`rev` event with different content is equivocation: it is rejected and alarmed. `created_at` never orders events of different `rev` (round 3).
   - Moderation is applied in the home's admission order, so a backdated `created_at` cannot reorder history. There is no timestamp backdating check beyond the relay's general admission window (round 3).
   - `created_at` is wall-clock time at signing and is never bumped. The home emits at most one snapshot per kind per group per second and coalesces bursts into the next second's snapshot. A snapshot is therefore never future-dated by a burst.
   - A removal (9001, ban, cohort removal) is published immediately, not on the next 30 s tick. "Immediately" means in the next one-second slot, which is inside the D margin of decision 4.
3. **Mirrors replicate verbatim and enforce locally.**
   - A mirror stores a 39000-39003 only if it is signed by the pinned home key, byte for byte, and replaces the stored one only under decision 2's order: a strictly greater `rev`, or an equal `rev` with a strictly greater `created_at` and identical content (a heartbeat). It never emits one of its own.
   - A mirror keeps a persisted watermark per group and per kind, holding `(rev, created_at, event id, content digest)`. The content digest is SHA-256 over the canonical JSON of the pair (`tags`, `content`), with `created_at`, id and signature excluded, so an equal-`rev` heartbeat is compared against it after a restart without persisting the membership list. It rejects any replay and any event that does not order strictly after the watermark under decision 2 (a lower `rev`, or an equal `rev` without a strictly greater `created_at` and identical content), including a late older one, and the watermark survives Durable Object eviction. After a restart it may rehydrate the exact accepted event (the same event id, signature verified again) to rebuild unchanged state, and never treats that rehydration as a newer revision. A per-kind watermark means a 39002 heartbeat never suppresses an older-`rev` 39000.
   - A mirror accepts kind 9 from members listed in the replicated 39002, applies the group's flags to reads and writes from replicated state, and forwards accepted kind 9 to the home.
   - Moderation 9000-9022 is accepted only at the home; a mirror forwards it.
4. **One revocation clock: D = 90 s (audit B2, round-2 C1).**
   - Constants, shared with VisionClaw `RoomAdmission`: snapshot lease L = 60 s; relative clock-error budget S = 10 s; live-session re-check interval C = 10 s.
   - The home re-signs 39002 at least every 30 s (L/2) even when nothing changed, at the current `rev` and subject to decision 10. It is driven by a Durable Object alarm, because the five-minute cron cannot meet it.
   - A mirror, and VisionClaw's `RoomAdmission`, treats a 39002 as fresh while `now − created_at ≤ L + S` (70 s) and stale after it, and refuses one with `created_at > now + S` as future-dated. Stale means deny for every membership-dependent action, including for sessions already joined, which are closed.
   - A mirror re-checks every live session against its current 39002 at least every C. Admission is also checked again after the session is registered, to close the race between a join and a removal.
   - The single guarantee is **D = 90 s**: from authoritative removal at the home to closure of every presence session and dataspace stream everywhere, with rejoin refused outright. It is a withheld removal (L + S = 70 s) plus C plus margin. Every earlier "within R" or "60 s" statement means D. Verification tests assert D. The snapshot becomes inadmissible after L + S; an existing session then closes at its next check, within C. **Voice is bound separately (final-audit ruling 7).** LiveKit cannot revoke an issued token, so a removed member may hold a token minted before their snapshot went stale (at most L + S after removal), valid for up to L more. Until then the reconciler removes each rejoin within C, so the member can reconnect for at most C at a time. The voice bound is **D_voice = 2L + 2S + C = 150 s** from removal at the home; after it, rejoin is impossible. **One further accepted exception:** during a LiveKit API partition, voice participants stay connected until the API returns (VisionClaw ADR-2160 D6, operator sign-off at activation); presence sessions and dataspace streams remain bound by D, and tests assert D_voice only with the API reachable.
5. **Transport is plain signed events.** Relays exchange events over NIP-42-authenticated `REQ` and `EVENT`, reusing `PeerManager`, the peer allowlists and `SeenIds` from `nostr-bbs-mesh`. NIP-77 negentropy catch-up follows in a later phase. The gift-wrapped IS-Envelope path is not used: it is point-to-point messaging, and a mirror must verify the original signature.
6. **The home signing key is separate and held in Worker secrets (audit M8).** The signing key is a different key from the administrator contact (`pubkey`) and from the relay-to-relay AUTH key. It is held in Worker secrets, never in the repository or `wrangler.toml`. It is rotated only by a deliberate re-home, which is a fork under decision 8. It is also not the VisionClaw node federation key, which agentbox holds (round-2 C5); the two never stand in for each other.
7. **Prerequisites, before any mirror stores an event.**
   - Replacement of 39000-39003 is ordered by `rev`, not `created_at`: a late older `rev` is rejected, an equal `rev` replaces only with a strictly greater `created_at` and identical content (decision 2), and an equal `rev` with different content is rejected, in the `ParameterizedReplaceable` branch of `storage.rs:119-139`. This is a D1 schema migration, not a guard patch: the events table has no `rev` column today, so the migration adds an indexed `rev` (parsed from the tag at admission) and the replacement query selects on it. The same `rev` migration covers exactly three further families (final-audit ruling 9): the NIP-53 30312 that ADR-2032 decision 4 publishes, and kind 30078 only where the `d` tag is in the `visionclaw:dataspace:*` namespace (the VisionClaw ADR-2161 catalogue, including its withdrawal replacements). For these a greater `rev` replaces, a lower `rev` is rejected, and an equal `rev` falls back to NIP-01 order (later `created_at`, then lowest id). Without this a timestamp-ordered relay could discard a higher-`rev` event, such as a same-second withdrawal, while keeping a lower one, and no consumer-side check recovers an event the relay dropped. Consumers still check `rev` themselves. Every other replaceable or addressable kind, including every other 30078 `d` tag such as `visionflow:claim:*`, keeps NIP-01 ordering (late older rejected, same-second ties by lowest id) in both branches of `storage.rs:97-139`. Today both branches only delete strictly older rows and insert with `ON CONFLICT (id) DO NOTHING`, so a late older event is stored beside the newer one. This is the forum prerequisite for decisions 2 and 3.
   - The dedup index and the watermark of decision 3 are persisted, not the in-memory `SeenIds` lost on Durable Object eviction.
   - The outbound Cloudflare `PeerConnector` is implemented.
   - Replicated 39000, 39001 and 39003 are exempt from the seven-day past bound, or a group unchanged for eight days cannot be mirrored. 39002 is re-signed every 30 s and needs no exemption.
   - Group kinds are admitted by the federation gate for the groups a relay homes or mirrors, not through the global default list alone.
8. **No move in v1; a fork is a new identity (audit M5).** There is no `moved_to` tag: the home never emits one, and clients and mirrors give one no meaning if they see it. A fork under a new home key is a new space with fresh membership; nothing carries over. A move needs an authenticated two-home handover (a frozen old revision, a destination snapshot, re-admission at the destination, revocation at cutover) and is a future ADR.
9. **The NIP-11 `nostr_bbs.federation` block lists homed and mirrored groups**, each with group id, role, home pubkey and home URL. A relay advertises no mirroring until prerequisite 7 holds.
10. **The home signs 39002 only while the projection is coherent (round-2 C2, audit finding 9).** For a zone-backed group (ADR-2030 decision 3), 39002 records `["proj", <cohort-rev>]`, the cohort revision the member set was projected from. The home signs a 39002, including the 30 s re-sign, only when the group's projection is coherent with the cohort authority's current revision. If the projection lags or fails, the home stops signing 39002 altogether. Consumers then go stale within L + S and deny, even though the signer and the network are healthy. A standalone group has no `proj` tag and is always coherent with its own stream.
11. **Activation order and gate (round-2 C9).** `depends_on` lists code dependencies only. Activation prerequisites are separate: this ADR needs ADR-2030 active, and VisionClaw `RoomAdmission` (ADR-2157) is activated after it. The forum chain is step 3 in the estate order: agentbox 2162; VisionClaw 2159 then 2161 for `public` and `dids`; forum 2030 then 2031; VisionClaw 2157 then 2161 for `space:`; VisionClaw 2160. VisionClaw 2161 `public` and `dids` never depend on this chain. The 30312 publisher of ADR-2032 decision 4 goes live with this chain at step 3 and is a prerequisite of step 5 (voice), not of `public` or `dids`.
12. **Federation ships dark too (operator, 2026-10-30).** `NIP29_FEDERATION_ENABLED` (Worker env, default `"false"`) switches homing announcements, mirroring, peer transport of group kinds and the NIP-11 federation block. It requires `NIP29_GROUPS_ENABLED` (ADR-2030 decision 12); a relay with federation on and groups off refuses to start. With it off, a relay runs local NIP-29 groups only. First use of federation is also the trigger for the deferred encrypted-zone decision (ADR-2030 decision 7).

## Consequences

- Easy: a second node can serve a group's readers and members near them; verification is a signature check against a pinned key plus a timestamp comparison.
- Hard: the home is a single point of ordering and failure. If the home is unreachable for longer than L + S, mirrors deny every membership-dependent action and close joined sessions; they do not serve private state from a stale snapshot. Re-signing 39002 every 30 s is a steady write load per homed group, and a cohort projection fault is now an outage for that group's remote consumers within 70 s by design. The only Durable Object alarm today is an idle-eviction handler (`relay_do/mod.rs:371`); a Durable Object holds one alarm, so the 30 s re-sign and C re-check must share it and stop the DO from idling out, which has a cost the first implementation must measure.
- The home key is a master identity for every space it homes: compromise lets an attacker sign state. No rotation or revocation exists short of a fork; ADR-074 D12 (key rotation announcement) was a follow-up and is not built. Staleness compares the home's `created_at` with the mirror's clock, so nodes must run NTP. Age is `now − created_at`, so a fast mirror clock makes snapshots look older and expire early (false denials), and a slow mirror clock delays age-based expiry by up to S; S is the budget inside D. Separately, a home whose clock runs more than S ahead of the mirror produces snapshots refused as future-dated. `created_at` need not rise with state: it is wall-clock time at signing, while state follows admission order, and the home's clock can step backwards (an NTP correction) between two snapshots. A client that orders by NIP-01 `(created_at, id)` alone can therefore pick the older state; `rev` is the authority, so only `rev`-aware consumers are covered by D. This does not contradict the emission limit of decision 2: at most one snapshot per kind per group per second.
- A forwarded member's kind 9 is admitted at the home by group membership. Today's admission checks the local whitelist for the event author (`nip_handlers.rs:1161`), which a mirror's member may not be on. How standalone-group members are admitted at the home is open.
- Foreclosed: multi-writer or active-active group state; mirrors that re-sign; identifying a space by host URL; trusting a home key on first use; moving a group with its membership in v1.
- Rejected: (a) hub-and-spoke through one forum relay (ADR-073 Alt-A): it makes a central trust root; (b) CRDT or multi-writer state: NIP-29 ordering is by one relay; (c) mirrors re-signing 39002: breaks the pin and lets any mirror forge membership; (d) gift-wrapped envelopes for sync: no verifiable original and a DM-shaped model; (e) a `moved_to` alias carried on 39000: unauthenticated by the destination and leaves old membership live; (f) ordering by `(created_at, id)` alone: a backdated moderation event rewrites history; (g) bumping `created_at` by a second on a tie (round 1): a burst outruns the future-date limit and turns fresh state into apparent backdating; (h) re-signing a lagging projection to keep it fresh: freshness would then say nothing about currency.
- Revisit if the home key is rotated or compromised, if NIP-77 changes, if a move is needed, if L, S, C or D change (they appear in ADR-2030 decision 5 and in VisionClaw `RoomAdmission`), or if ADR-2013 in VisionClaw ends its federation deferral.

## Archived rationale (not authority)

Relied on from the archive and restated here rather than deferred to. The records are in the VisionClaw repository (`DreamLab-AI/VisionClaw`), `docs/archive/adr/`, read in its `ontomark/parse` worktree at commit `cc29b82be59b0354119a93d110213f3edc765260`. They are not in this repository's archive.
- ADR-073 D1: relays federate as mutual subscribers with no hub and no central trust root. The home here is a per-group authority, not a mesh hub.
- ADR-073 D3: writes need NIP-42 AUTH; reads of 30000-39999 are open except moderation. Hence ADR-2030 decision 4.
- ADR-073 D4 and ADR-080 D2, D6: a relay holds a federation key, distinct from its operator key, for relay-to-relay AUTH; peers admit each other bilaterally through `allowed_remote_dids`. The group-signing key pinned here is a third role and is not that AUTH key.
- ADR-073 D9 and ADR-075 D12: dedup by event id, capacity 4096, TTL 600 s; this ADR keeps the bounds and persists them.
- ADR-074 D1 (retained): pubkeys are lowercase 64-hex on every surface.
- Status: ADR-073 and ADR-080 are frozen as Deferred (2026-07-03), ADR-075 is Proposed, and ADR-074 D1 is retained.
